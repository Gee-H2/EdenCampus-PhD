# Editorial Review — Paper 1: Multi-Vector Digital Twin Framework for Eden Campus

**Target journal:** Applied Energy
**Review type:** Narrative scholarly review (argument, evidence, internal consistency)
**Manuscript:** `Paper1_Eden_Campus_Manuscript.docx`
**Cross-checked against:** `full_year_scenario_comparison.xlsx` (authoritative annual KPIs), `sensitivity_analysis.xlsx`, `DATA_RECONCILIATION.md`, `REVISED_TEXT.md`, figure caption list.

---

## Summary verdict

The paper is a well-structured, clearly argued framework/methodology study with a genuinely interesting central result (the tariff-dependent heat-pump peak finding). The spine of the argument — S1 as operationally optimal — follows cleanly from the authoritative KPI table and is internally self-consistent across the abstract, Table 3, Table 4, and the Discussion. The recent cost-unit correction has largely landed: Table 3 and Table 4 agree to the decimal, and the headline £71k/£1.42M savings reconcile exactly with the capacity-charge cost column.

However, the manuscript is **not yet submission-ready**. The cost-unit fix did not propagate completely: one Results paragraph still carries stale energy-only cost figures that directly contradict the paper's own Table 3, and two grid-import values in Table 3 do not match the authoritative model output. A reviewer who tabulates the numbers — and at Applied Energy they will — will find these within fifteen minutes and lose trust in the whole results section. Separately, the synthetic-demand limitation is disclosed honestly but is undercut by residual "measured data" / "real-time" framing elsewhere in the paper, and the entire reference apparatus is a placeholder. These are fixable in a focused revision pass, but they are blocking as the manuscript stands.

**Verdict: NEEDS_CHANGES** (major revision before submission; no re-modelling required, but a numbers-reconciliation and framing pass is mandatory).

---

## 1. Argument coherence

The core claim holds. The abstract states S1 is "operationally optimal," and this is exactly what the authoritative table supports: S1 has the lowest annual cost (£468.0k vs £539.0k baseline), the largest cost saving (£71.0k/yr), the second-highest CO₂ saving (217.2 t/yr), and a strong peak reduction (39.7%). The claim is correctly qualified as *operational* throughout — the paper is careful to say it excludes capital cost, and defers the full techno-economic case to later work. That restraint is appropriate and will read well to a reviewer. **(confirmed)**

The tariff-dependent heat-pump finding is the paper's strongest contribution and the argument is logically sound: under energy-only billing the optimiser has no incentive to shave peak, so heat-pump load piles onto winter evening peaks (+35%); under a capacity charge the optimiser is paid to manage peak, so S4's co-located storage reverses it. The effective-cost mechanism (£280/MWh ÷ COP 3 = £93/MWh < £110/MWh gas) is a clean, quantified causal explanation. This is a legitimately novel, practitioner-relevant result and it is the right thing to foreground. **(confirmed)**

One coherence weakness: the paper claims S1 has "no peak demand penalty" (abstract, §6.1), but the authoritative capacity-charge model gives S1 a **39.7% peak *reduction***, not merely the absence of a penalty. "No penalty" is defensible but badly understates the result and sits awkwardly next to the §6.2 story that the capacity charge actively rewards peak management. Under the paper's own primary tariff, S1 is a peak-reducer; say so. **(confirmed)**

## 2. Internal consistency — the blocking issues

Table 3 and Table 4 are mutually consistent: every annual-saving entry in Table 4 equals S0-minus-scenario from Table 3 cost column (S1 +71.0, S2 −144.3, S3 +23.4, S4 −89.4), and the 20-year columns are exactly ×20. The abstract's £71k/£1.42M and the §5.1/§6.1 prose agree with this. That part of the reconciliation is clean. **(confirmed)**

But three numeric contradictions remain, and they are the kind a referee will catch:

**(a) Stale energy-only costs in Results §5.1 (para 108) — most damaging.** The prose states "substantially higher annual operational costs (**£600.2k for S2 and £564.2k for S4**; Figure 4, panel f)." Table 3 — in the same section — gives **£683.3k (S2) and £628.4k (S4)**. These are the old energy-only numbers that survived the cost-unit correction. A reader comparing the sentence to the table two paragraphs up sees an internal contradiction in the headline results. Worse, the prose says S4 (£564.2k) is *cheaper* than S2 (£600.2k), while Table 3 says the same ordering but at different absolute values — the stale pair happens to preserve the ordering, which makes the error easy to miss on a casual read and damning on a careful one. **(confirmed — fix: replace with £683.3k / £628.4k)**

**(b) Table 3 grid-import column disagrees with the authoritative model for two scenarios. (confirmed)**

| Scenario | Table 3 (manuscript) | Authoritative `full_year_scenario_comparison.xlsx` | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| S2: PV+PCM TES | 2,142.6 MWh | **2,122.2 MWh** | +20.4 |
| S4: Full Hybrid | 2,013.6 MWh | **1,820.1 MWh** | +193.5 |

S0/S1/S3 match (1,636.9 / 1,485.6→1,495.6¹ / 1,573.2≈1,573.3). The S4 discrepancy is nearly 194 MWh — ~10% — which is not a rounding artefact and suggests the grid-import column was populated from a different (pre-correction) model run than the cost column. Since grid import drives both the CO₂ and cost KPIs, a referee will ask why the import number doesn't reconcile with the cost. Regenerate the whole Table 3 from the single authoritative run.

¹ Table 3 shows S1 import 1,485.6; authoritative is 1,495.6 — a 10 MWh / transposition-looking mismatch. Minor but same root cause.

**(c) Peak-change percentage drifts between sections. (confirmed)** The energy-only peak rise is quoted as **35%** in the abstract, highlights, §1.3, and §6.2 (671.9→904.4 kW = +34.6%, correctly rounds to 35%), but the **Conclusions (para 146) say "approximately 33%."** Pick 35% and make it uniform. Relatedly, the capacity-charge S4 peak reduction is "~40%" in §6.2 (authoritative 40.0%, good) but the `REVISED_TEXT.md` abstract draft says "up to 39%" — ensure whichever abstract wording ships uses 40% to match the model, or states the S1 figure (39.7%) explicitly if that's the intended "up to 39%."

None of these require re-running the model (the authoritative file already has the right values) — they require regenerating the table and prose from that one file and deleting every hand-carried legacy number.

## 3. Evidence adequacy

Where the numbers are correct, they are backed by model output: solar fractions (31.8/38.8/35.0/39.4/42.7), CO₂ savings, and the sensitivity figures (BESS knee at 2,000 kWh ≈ 42.3–42.4%, 250kW/1000kWh ≡ 500kW/1000kWh at 40.2%, weather-year ±2pp, grid-price invariance) all reconcile with `sensitivity_analysis.xlsx`. The representative-vs-full-year bias (S1 42.4% vs 38.8%; S4 47.3% vs 42.7%) is self-consistent and the magnitude claim (2.2–4.6 pp) is internally coherent. **(confirmed)**

Two evidence gaps a referee will flag:

- **Every citation is a `[REF]` placeholder, and the References section is a stub** ("[References to be compiled… approximately 40–50 references]"). This is normal for an internal draft but cannot go to Applied Energy — the literature-gap claims in §1.2 and §2 ("previously under-reported," "to the authors' knowledge the first") are unsupportable without the citations that would establish the gap. The novelty claim literally depends on references that aren't there. **(confirmed — blocking for submission, not for internal review)**

- **The 2,142.6/2,013.6 grid-import values in Table 3 are not reproducible from the committed authoritative file** (see §2b). Any quantified claim that can't be regenerated from the published repo undermines the paper's own reproducibility selling point. **(confirmed)**

Author/affiliation placeholders (`[Co-author 1]`, `[email]`) and the funding-acknowledgement placeholder are also still present. Housekeeping, but they signal "draft" to an editor.

## 4. Limitations honesty — adequate but undercut

The synthetic-demand limitation is handled about as well as it can be for a framework paper. §4.3 states plainly "In the absence of metered hourly site data, electrical and thermal demand profiles were synthesised… using CIBSE Guide F," gives the methodology (baseload-plus-peak, seasonal scaling, weekday/weekend), and §6.5 lists it as the highest-severity limitation with a concrete remediation path (sub-metering validation). That framing — synthetic-but-transparent, reproducible, cross-site-comparable — is a legitimate defence for a methodology contribution and an Applied Energy reviewer will accept it *provided it is consistent*. **(confirmed, good)**

The problem is that it is **not consistent with the rest of the paper**, and this is exactly the open item flagged in `DATA_RECONCILIATION.md`:

- The abstract calls the framework's first layer "real-time data acquisition." §3.1 and Figure 2/3 captions describe "measured where available" and a "bidirectional feedback loop… to real-world site data." The §4.3 admission that no site data was used contradicts the digital-twin framing that the abstract and architecture sections lean on. A sharp reviewer will say: *this is not a digital twin, it is a simulation with synthetic inputs* — because the defining DT property (bidirectional coupling to measured data) that §2.3 itself names is absent in the actual study. **(confirmed — likely to draw a pointed referee comment)**

This will not be *fatal* if reconciled: soften the DT claims to "a digital-twin-ready framework" / "demonstrated with synthetic inputs pending instrumentation," and make the abstract/architecture language match §4.3. Left as-is, the gap between "real-time / measured" up front and "no metered data" in §4.3 is the single most likely objection after the numeric errors. The reconciliation note's STILL-OPEN item on this is correct and must be closed before submission.

Minor: §4.3 cites thermal annual as 1,728 MWh (matches the modelled 1,727.6 MWh and the reconciliation fix), but `REVISED_TEXT.md` still shows the old 1,648 MWh in its draft sentence — confirm the shipped manuscript uses 1,728 (the extracted text does, so this is fine; just don't re-import the stale REVISED_TEXT value).

## 5. Figure / caption alignment

Captions are plausible against the model output and mostly act as a faithful contract:

- **Figure 4** caption claims heat-pump scenarios "do not increase peak grid demand" under the capacity-charge tariff. This matches the authoritative peak-reduction column (S2 1.4%, S4 40.0% — i.e. no increase). Consistent. **(confirmed)**
- **Figure 6** caption's "250 kW/1,000 kWh and 500 kW/1,000 kWh achieve identical KPIs" matches the sensitivity sheet exactly (both 40.2%, £31.3k). **(confirmed)**
- **Figure 1, 5, 7** captions describe seasonal-mismatch, monthly-breakdown, and S4 multi-vector dispatch content that is consistent with the monthly sheets and scenario design. Plausible. **(confirmed)**

Two alignment risks:

- **Figure 4 panel (f) vs §5.1 prose.** The caption and panel (f) are "annual operational cost." If panel (f) was plotted from the authoritative Table-3 costs (£683.3k/£628.4k) but the §5.1 prose cites £600.2k/£564.2k, the figure and the text that references it disagree. Confirm panel (f) was regenerated on the capacity-charge basis and fix the prose to match. **(likely — same root cause as §2a; verify against the figure PNG before submission)**
- **Figure 4 panel (e) "peak demand reduction relative to S0."** Under the primary capacity-charge tariff this should show S1≈40%, S4≈40%, S2≈0%. The abstract's "no peak penalty" framing for S1 must not contradict a panel that visibly shows S1 at ~40% reduction. Align the narrative with what the panel plots. **(likely)**

The caption-level "contract" is otherwise sound; the risk is entirely that figures were regenerated from the corrected run while some prose was not.

1. **Stale energy-only costs in §5.1 (para 108)** — prose says S2 £600.2k / S4 £564.2k; Table 3 says £683.3k / £628.4k. Replace with the Table-3 values. *Blocking.*
2. **Table 3 grid-import mismatch (S2, S4)** — 2,142.6 vs 2,122.2 and 2,013.6 vs 1,820.1 (authoritative). Regenerate Table 3 entirely from `full_year_scenario_comparison.xlsx`; also fix S1 1,485.6→1,495.6. *Blocking.*
3. **Peak-change % inconsistent** — abstract/highlights/§6.2 say 35%, Conclusions say 33%. Standardise on 35% (energy-only); use 40% for the S4 capacity-charge reduction. *Blocking.*
4. **"No peak penalty" understates S1** — under the primary capacity-charge tariff S1 gives a 39.7% peak *reduction*; restate accordingly in abstract and §6.1.
5. **DT framing vs synthetic data** — abstract "real-time data acquisition" and §3.1/Fig 2–3 "measured / bidirectional" contradict §4.3's "no metered data." Soften to "digital-twin-ready / demonstrated with synthetic inputs." Closes the open reconciliation item. *Blocking for submission.*
6. **All citations are `[REF]` placeholders; References section is a stub** — novelty/gap claims are unsupported until ~40–50 references are inserted. *Blocking for submission.*
7. **Figure 4 panel (f) cost / panel (e) peak** — verify both were plotted on the capacity-charge basis so they match the corrected Table 3 and the S1-peak narrative; fix prose if not.
8. **Author/affiliation/funding placeholders** — `[Co-author 1]`, `[email]`, funding line must be completed.
9. **Don't re-import stale `REVISED_TEXT.md` values** — thermal 1,648 MWh and energy-only cost pairs in that draft are superseded; the shipped manuscript's 1,728 MWh and capacity-charge costs are the correct basis.

## Would an Applied Energy reviewer recommend it?

As submitted today: **major revision** at best, with a real risk of a reject-resubmit if the numeric contradictions and the DT-framing gap land badly with a quantitative referee. The science and the headline findings are sound and the capacity-charge heat-pump result is publishable and interesting. But the paper currently reads as a draft caught mid-correction: the cost-unit fix reached the tables but not all the prose, grid-import values weren't regenerated from the authoritative run, and the digital-twin positioning overpromises relative to the synthetic-data reality.

Fix the nine items above — none require new modelling, only reconciliation against the one authoritative file plus a citation pass — and this becomes a competitive major-revision-ready submission.
