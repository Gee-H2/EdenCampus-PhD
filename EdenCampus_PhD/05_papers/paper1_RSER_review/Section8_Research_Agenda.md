# Section 8 — Research Agenda

Purpose (skeleton): six gaps, each mapped to a concrete next output.

The synthesis (Section 7) identifies a coherent set of gaps between the latent
flexibility capability of UK business parks and its realisation. Six are set out
as a research agenda, each mapped to a concrete next output.

**G1 — Estate-scale flexibility service provider (FSP) participation models.**
The provider-side question left open by Section 5's D6 gap: how a multi-tenant
estate registers, aggregates across MPANs, and participates as an FSP in local and
national markets. Needs empirical participation modelling and governance design.
*Output:* estate-FSP participation framework [REF].

**G2 — Co-optimised electrical–thermal dispatch under live product constraints.**
Section 6 shows the products; Section 7 shows that idealised dispatch mis-specifies
value. The gap is dispatch optimisation that co-optimises electrical and thermal
vectors *under real market/product constraints and a system-aware objective*
(avoiding the objective-function trap). *Output:* **Paper 2**.

**G3 — Peak-aware thermal storage with melt band as a decision variable.**
The heat-pump peak bivalence (Section 7.2) motivates thermal-storage control that
treats the phase-change melt band as an explicit decision variable for peak
management, not a fixed parameter. *Output:* **Paper 3**.

**G4 — Typology-transferable twin architectures and a minimum data specification.**
Section 7.5's single-estate limitation and Section 7.4's capability-ladder
precondition require digital-twin architectures that transfer across the typology
(T1–T5), with a defined minimum data specification for participation. *Output:*
thesis **Objective V** [REF].

**G5 — Revenue stacking under MHHS and P415.**
Section 6's reform layer opens new stacking opportunities whose value at estate
scale is unquantified. The gap is quantified revenue-stacking analysis under the
post-MHHS, post-P415 arrangements. *Output:* revenue-stacking study [REF].

**G6 — Governance instruments for estate flexibility.**
Section 7's governance-fragmentation barrier requires instruments to resolve the
split-incentive problem: green leases, ESCo structures, and — drawing on the
Section 6 NI comparator — the case for a **GB estate participation category
modelled on the SEM Demand Side Unit**. *Output:* governance-instruments analysis
and a policy proposal [REF].

Together the agenda moves from the review's diagnosis to a programme that
addresses provider participation (G1, G5), system-aware multi-vector dispatch
(G2, G3), transferable capability (G4), and enabling governance and market design
(G6) — with G2 and G3 constituting the immediate successor modelling papers for
which this review is the foundation.
