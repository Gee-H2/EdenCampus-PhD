## 3.1 Protocol and reporting standard

The review follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and
Meta-Analyses (PRISMA 2020) statement [REF: PRISMA 2020, Page et al.], extended
with PRISMA-S for the reporting of the literature-search process and, given the
central role of policy and regulatory documents in this field, PRISMA-S guidance
on grey-literature searching [REF: PRISMA-S, Rethlefsen et al.]. The protocol was
defined in advance [VERIFY: register or state "not registered" — RSER does not
require PROSPERO registration, but state the position explicitly]. The review
question is: *to what extent, and with what evidence, are UK business parks
recognised and enabled as providers of electricity-system flexibility?*

## 3.2 Databases and search window

Peer-reviewed literature was searched across three databases: **Scopus, Web of
Science Core Collection, and IEEE Xplore** [REF]. The search window is
**1 January 2010 to 31 August 2026**, chosen to capture the period over which
distributed flexibility, DSO transition, and estate-scale energy systems emerged
as active research areas [VERIFY: confirm the end date matches your actual search
date].

## 3.3 Search strategy

Seven Boolean search strings were constructed, one per literature stream
(Section 5), combining a *business-park/estate* concept block with a
*flexibility/market* concept block. Reporting one string per stream — rather than
a single omnibus query — keeps the mapping between search and synthesis auditable
and is presented in full in Table 2. [DATA: insert the seven finalised strings and
per-string hit counts.]

A named grey-literature corpus supplements the database search, reflecting that
much of the market and regulatory evidence in this field is published by system
bodies rather than in journals. The corpus comprises: **Ofgem, NESO, the Energy
Networks Association (ENA), DESNZ, SP Energy Networks (SPEN), SSEN, SONI, and
Piclo** [VERIFY: confirm the final list — note SSEN is the relevant DNO for the
Fife/Eden Campus exemplar, not SPEN; include both if GB-wide coverage is intended].
Grey sources were identified by structured searching of each body's publication
library and are reported per PRISMA-S [REF].

## 3.4 Screening

Records were de-duplicated and screened in **Rayyan** [REF: Ouzzani et al., Rayyan].
Title-and-abstract screening applied the inclusion criteria (UK or
UK-transferable; estate/park or directly relevant flexibility scale; within the
window). A **10% dual-screening** sample was independently assessed by a second
screener, with inter-rater agreement reported as **Cohen's κ** [REF]; disagreements
were resolved by discussion. [DATA: report κ value and n dual-screened.]

## 3.5 Quality appraisal

Included studies were appraised on three field-specific dimensions rather than a
generic checklist:
1. **Data transparency** — whether inputs, assumptions, and code are disclosed and
   reproducible;
2. **Scale realism** — whether the modelled scale matches a real estate rather than
   a single building or a national abstraction;
3. **Market realism** — whether real market/regulatory constraints (products,
   tariffs, participation rules) are represented rather than idealised.

These dimensions are chosen because they are precisely where estate-scale
flexibility studies most often fall short, and they feed directly into the gap
analysis of Section 5. [REF: justify the appraisal dimensions.]

## 3.6 Managing author self-citation

Because this review sets up the authors' own subsequent modelling work, and to
guard against self-citation bias, **all candidate self-citations were assessed by
the second screener** against the same inclusion and appraisal criteria applied to
external studies. This is stated explicitly for transparency and is noted again as
a limitation in Section 7.4.
